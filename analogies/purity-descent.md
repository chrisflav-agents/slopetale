## Question

Does Mathlib already have, under a different name, the lemma:

> Let `φ : R →+* S` be a flat ring map of commutative rings. Let `J ⊆ S` be
> a pure ideal of `S` (i.e. `S/J` is flat as `S`-module). Then the
> contraction `φ⁻¹(J) ⊆ R` is a pure ideal of `R`.

in `blueprint/src/chapters/more-on-local-structure.tex`, with target Lean

### Decision: is the lemma TRUE as stated?

I did not find a counterexample, but I also could not verify the proof.
Two specific problems with the blueprint's 12-line diagram-chase proof
at `more-on-local-structure.tex:754-774`:

1. **The right vertical `M → S ⊗_R M` is claimed injective "by flatness
   of φ".** This is wrong. Flatness alone is not enough — it requires
   *faithful* flatness. Counterexample: `R = ℤ`, `S = ℚ` (flat, not
   faithfully flat), `M = ℤ/n`, then `M → ℚ ⊗ ℤ/n = 0` is not injective.
2. **The left vertical `φ⁻¹(J) ⊗_R M → J ⊗_S (S ⊗_R M)` is said to
   "factor through" because `φ⁻¹(J)` is the contraction of `J`.** No
   factorization is actually established. The natural candidate is the
   composition `φ⁻¹(J) ⊗_R M → φ(φ⁻¹(J)) · S ⊗_S (S ⊗_R M) → J ⊗_S
   (S ⊗_R M)`, but the second map there is induced by the inclusion
   `φ(φ⁻¹(J)) · S ↪ J` (which is in general a strict inclusion, not
   equality) and is not obviously injective.

I attempted the element-wise approach via the local characterization
of pure ideals (`x ∈ I ⟹ ∃ y ∈ I, x = xy`, Stacks 04PT) and
`Ann_S(φ(x)) = S · φ(Ann_R(x))` (Mathlib has the latter via
flatness-and-annihilators). The reduction goes: given `x ∈ φ⁻¹(J)`,
purity of `J` gives `y ∈ J` with `φ(x)(1-y) = 0`, so `1-y ∈ S·φ(Ann_R x)`.
The descent step "express `1 - z mod J` as an `R`-linear combination
of `φ(Ann_R(x))`" requires descending coefficients from `S` to `R`
modulo `J`, which does **not** follow from flatness alone. It would
follow from faithful flatness + `J = φ(φ⁻¹(J))·S` (i.e. `J` is the
extension of its contraction), but the directive's hypotheses give
neither.

For the *project's* concrete use (`R = L'⊗_K L'`, `S = L⊗_K L`,
`φ = canonical inclusion`, `J = I_L`), the map `φ` is in fact
faithfully flat (since `L/L'` is a field extension, `L` is free over
`L'`, so `L⊗_K L` is free over `L'⊗_K L'`). But `J = I_L` is **not**
the extension of `I_{L'}`: `I_L` is the diagonal ideal of `L⊗_K L`
generated by `{1⊗a − a⊗1 : a ∈ L}`, while `φ(I_{L'}) · (L⊗_K L)`
is only generated by `{1⊗b − b⊗1 : b ∈ L'}`, a strictly smaller
ideal. So even strengthening to faithful flatness does not rescue
the diagram chase.

- **Mathlib idiom (for confirming TRUTH)**: none — Mathlib does not
  cite this lemma anywhere.
- **Project's current path**: take the lemma on faith from a
  10-line blueprint sketch.
- **Gap**: divergent-and-wrong (or at least, divergent-and-unverified).
- **Verdict**: not a Mathlib alignment question — this is a
  mathematical-soundness question that should be escalated.

## Recommendation

The carrier `Ideal.IsPure.of_flat_extension` as currently stated in
`blueprint/src/chapters/more-on-local-structure.tex:745-751` is **not
present in Mathlib in any form** I could locate, and the blueprint's
proof sketch is broken in at least two places (faithful-flatness
confusion + unjustified left-vertical factorization). The element-wise
descent reduction also breaks in a way that cannot be repaired by
strengthening "flat" to "faithfully flat" in the directive setting
(`I_L` is not the extension of `I_{L'}`).

Recommended actions, in priority order:

1. **Specialize, do not generalize.** Replace the staged general
   purity-descent lemma with a specialized statement tailored to the
   project's actual setting: "if `K → L` is weakly étale, `L` is a
   field, `L' = K(a') ⊆ L` is a finitely generated `K`-subfield, then
   `K → L'` is weakly étale". The specialized statement may be
   provable directly via the structure of `L⊗_K L` as a free
   `L'⊗_K L'`-module (faithful flatness) **plus** the fact that
   `I_L = I_{L'} · (L⊗_K L) + $ correction terms`, where the
   correction terms vanish under the multiplication map. This is the
   route that actually exploits the project-specific structure rather
   than discarding it via an over-general carrier.

2. **Cross-check the general claim.** Before any prover iter touches
   `Ideal.IsPure.of_flat_extension`, dispatch a literature-fetching
   subagent (e.g. the `WebSearch` route the project uses for
   Sharp–Wadsworth and Hiblot) to confirm whether the unconditional
   "contraction of pure along flat is pure" appears anywhere in the
   commutative-algebra literature (Lazard's "Autour de la platitude",
   Stenström's "Rings of Quotients", Bourbaki Comm. Alg. ch. I §2).
   If it does, the project can cite the paper proof; if it does not
   (likely), discard the lemma entirely and pivot to the specialized
   route in #1.

3. **Do not stage a Lean prover dispatch on the lemma as written.**
   The iter-148 prover plan should be edited to remove
   `Ideal.IsPure.of_flat_extension` from the prover queue. Staging a
   prover on a mathematically-unsound carrier wastes the prover slot
   and pollutes `proof-journal` with attempts that cannot succeed.
